# Product Planning – OneSync Website

## 1. Purpose of the Website

The OneSync website is not a marketing-only site. It is a **product surface** that explains trust, architecture, and value clearly to three audiences:

- Athletes (privacy, control, safety)
- Teams / coaches (insight, reliability, decision support)
- Engineers / partners (architecture, rigor, feasibility)

The website should communicate that OneSync is a **system**, not an app, and that it enforces intelligence, privacy, and discipline across hardware, mobile, and cloud.

---

## 2. Primary Goals

1. Explain the OneBand + OneSync system at a conceptual level
2. Build trust around privacy, consent, and non-diagnostic boundaries
3. Demonstrate technical depth without overwhelming
4. Act as a foundation for future dashboards, demos, and investor material

---

## 3. Target User Personas

### Athlete
- Wants to know: What is collected? What is not? Who controls it?
- Key concern: Privacy, misuse, surveillance
- Desired outcome: Trust and transparency

### Coach / Performance Staff
- Wants to know: What insights are reliable? When should I trust them?
- Key concern: False signals, overconfidence, legal risk
- Desired outcome: Clear, conditional insights with context

### Technical Stakeholder (Engineer / Research / Partner)
- Wants to know: How this is built and why it is defensible
- Key concern: Feasibility, rigor, scalability
- Desired outcome: Respect for the architecture

---

## 4. Core Pages (v1)

### 4.1 Home
- High-level system explanation
- OneBand + OneSync mental model
- Core principles: Edge-first, conditional inference, privacy

### 4.2 System Overview
- Visual breakdown of OneBand → OneSync Mobile → OneSync Cloud
- What runs where and why
- Data flow and trust boundaries

### 4.3 OneBand (Device)
- What it senses
- What it computes on-device
- Why raw data is not streamed

### 4.4 OneSync (Platform)
- Mobile role (consent, encryption, offload)
- Cloud role (dashboards, trends, models)
- Policy enforcement and auditability

### 4.5 Insights (Non-Medical)
- Stress Load Index
- Cognitive Load Proxy
- Recovery and readiness trends
- Emphasis on confidence and NO INFERENCE states

### 4.6 Privacy & Governance
- Consent model
- What is never stored
- Athlete-first data ownership

### 4.7 Technology & Architecture
- Edge computing philosophy
- Feature-first storage
- OTA, security, and reliability

---

## 5. Information Hierarchy

Level 1: Concepts and trust
Level 2: System behavior and guarantees
Level 3: Technical depth and validation

Users should be able to stop at any level and still feel informed.

---

## 6. Tone and Language

- Calm
- Confident
- Precise
- Never sensational

Avoid medical claims, hype language, or exaggerated AI promises.

---

## 7. Future Extensions

- Interactive system diagrams
- Live demo dashboards (mock data)
- Athlete consent simulator
- Research and validation pages

---

## 8. Success Criteria

- Users understand what the system does and does not do
- Privacy model is clearly understood
- Architecture feels intentional and mature
- The site feels like infrastructure, not marketing
