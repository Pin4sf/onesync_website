# Design & UI/UX – OneSync Website

## 1. Design Philosophy

The OneSync website should feel like **infrastructure you can trust**.

Not flashy. Not noisy. Not consumer-gimmicky.

Design should communicate:
- Control
- Discipline
- Safety
- Intelligence

The UI should feel closer to aerospace or medical systems than fitness apps.

---

## 2. Visual Identity

### Color Palette

- Primary: Deep graphite / near-black
- Secondary: Cool neutral grays
- Accent: Muted blue or cyan (used sparingly)
- Status colors: Green (valid), Amber (limited), Gray (no inference)

No aggressive reds or neon colors.

---

## 3. Typography

- Headings: Clean geometric sans-serif
- Body: Highly readable sans-serif
- Code / technical snippets: Monospace

Typography hierarchy must clearly separate:
- Concepts
- Guarantees
- Technical detail

---

## 4. Layout Principles

- Large margins and whitespace
- Strong alignment
- Predictable grids
- Slow, deliberate scrolling experience

Nothing should feel rushed or crowded.

---

## 5. Core UI Patterns

### 5.1 System Diagrams

- Minimal line diagrams
- Clear directional arrows
- Explicit boundaries (device, mobile, cloud)
- Labels like "Computed on device" and "Never leaves device"

These diagrams are more important than illustrations.

---

### 5.2 Insight Cards

Insight cards should include:
- Name of the index
- What it represents (plain language)
- When it is available
- When it is not available

Explicitly show "NO INFERENCE" states as a valid outcome.

---

### 5.3 Confidence Indicators

Every insight visualization should include:
- Signal quality indicator
- Confidence level
- Active modalities used

Confidence is not hidden. It is foregrounded.

---

## 6. UX Principles

### 6.1 Honesty Over Completeness

If the system cannot infer something, the UI should say so clearly.
No placeholders. No fake numbers.

---

### 6.2 Progressive Disclosure

- First layer: Simple explanation
- Second layer: Why it works
- Third layer: How it is built

Advanced users can go deep. Others are not forced to.

---

### 6.3 Athlete-Centric Controls

Consent and control should feel:
- Simple
- Reversible
- Explicit

UI should emphasize that the athlete is in control.

---

## 7. Motion and Interaction

- Subtle transitions only
- No distracting animations
- Diagrams may animate slowly to explain flow

Motion should teach, not impress.

---

## 8. Accessibility

- High contrast text
- Clear font sizes
- Color is never the only signal

Status and confidence must be readable without color.

---

## 9. Mobile vs Desktop

### Desktop
- Primary platform
- Used for explanation, demos, and dashboards

### Mobile
- Focused on reading and trust
- No dense dashboards

---

## 10. What to Avoid

- Fitness influencer aesthetics
- Overly emotional language
- Gamification
- Medical-style alarmism
- Black-box AI visuals

---

## 11. Desired User Feeling

After browsing the site, a user should feel:

"This system is careful, deliberate, and built by people who understand the risks."

That feeling matters more than conversion.
